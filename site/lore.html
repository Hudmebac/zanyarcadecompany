Chapter One

The Splintering

How one fire became six kingdoms, why their wars are so small, and what the Compact has to say about all of it.

In this chapter

  2. Six Sparks, Six Hearths
  3. The Core Compact

I. The First Hearth

Before the kingdoms there was one fire, and it was very large and very bored. It had been burning since before there was anything to burn, which left it with a great deal of time and nobody to tell about it.

So the fire told itself stories. Most of them were about weather. One of them, on a particularly long evening, was about company.

The story went like this: somewhere out in the dark there ought to be someone else at a fire, arguing about whether the logs were arranged properly. The fire thought this was the best idea it had ever had. It was so pleased that six sparks leapt clean out of the flames to see whether the story was any good.

They were not asked to leave. They were not asked to come back either. They simply went, which is how most of the important things in the world begin.

II. Six Sparks, Six Hearths

Each spark fell where it fell, and each one immediately set about making the place comfortable.

One landed in a warm caldera and decided that a volcano would make an excellent front room. One landed in a mirror-lake and spent a week apologising to the tide. One landed on a lumpy mountain, sat down in the mud, and did not get up for a season. One landed in a pile of clouds, which is not really landing at all, and has been mildly smug about it ever since. One landed on a golden staircase and found the porch light already on. One landed in a crystal hollow under the world, where it discovered purple fireflies and a secret snack, and chose to keep both.

This is the Splintering, and it was not a war, whatever the songs say. It was a fire learning to share.

Six sparks, six hearths, six opinions about the correct way to arrange logs. The opening line of every history of the Little Wars

III. The Core Compact

The kingdoms discovered very quickly that they had opinions, and that opinions travel. First came the Argument of the Shared River. Then the Dispute of the Very Good Mud. Then, after a generation of increasingly inventive rudeness, the War of the Long Muddle — a conflict so muddled that nobody remembers who won, because everybody did and nobody did.

Afterwards, the six rulers met on a neutral hill and wrote the Core Compact on the back of an extremely large leaf. The Compact is short, because it was written by people who wanted to go home for dinner. It says:

  • One. No kingdom may be unmade. There are six hearths and there will go on being six hearths.
  • Two. Disagreements are settled in the Little Wars, at an agreed place, with an agreed number of platforms.
  • Three. Every kingdom builds a gatehouse and a core. One hundred and twenty is a polite number for a gate; three hundred and sixty is a polite number for a core. These are the Compact's two numbers and nobody is allowed to be clever about them.
  • Four. A core that pops is a concession, not a casualty. The Pips rebuild it overnight. The Pips are unionised and will not be hurried.
  • Five. No jumping. It has been tried. It only encourages showing off.

IV. How a Little War Is Fought

A Little War is a ritual with a scoreboard. Each kingdom opens its spawn gate — a Lava Vent, a Tidewell, a Mud Burrow, a Cloud Chute, a Sun Gate, a Shade Well — and sends out its pals in three familiar shapes. A Pip is cheap and bouncy. A Chonk is large and unbothered. A Zap stands back and throws things. They cost spark, which every kingdom generates simply by being extremely itself.

Pal meets pal. Gatehouse meets gatehouse. The Compact prefers that the core is cracked open last, so that the losing side has time to compose a dignified statement about the mud, the tides, or the general unfairness of clouds.

The rest of the ritual depends entirely on which kingdom is hosting. Some prefer straight lanes and a clean race. Some insist on a maze, and then insist on moving the walls, because a settled maze is a maze with no conversation in it. Some build towers and hold a siege. Some put three neutral towers on the field and let the towers themselves decide who is winning.

All of it is theatre. None of it is a grudge. The kingdoms take the wars very seriously and the results not at all.

V. The Final Bounce

Every Little War ends the same way. At two minutes and forty-five seconds, the sky makes an announcement. Every gate still standing splits down the middle, both cores are exposed, and the last stretch becomes a flat race to the poppable bit.

The kingdoms call it the Final Bounce and pretend it is a great hardship. In private, they adore it. It is the only part of the Compact that everyone remembers word for word, and the only part that was written by the fire itself.

Then the Pips sweep up, the gatehouses are rebuilt, and somebody suggests a rematch. Somebody always suggests a rematch.

Chapter Two

The Six Kingdoms

Each kingdom keeps a hearth, a homeland, a front gate and one very small mascot with very strong opinions.

Kingdom I · Fire

Volcano Forge

“Where every spark gets a warm welcome.”

Built inside a caldera that the residents insist is merely resting, Volcano Forge is the loudest, warmest and most competitive hearth in the six. Its lava fountain is decorative. Its lava fountain is also, occasionally, not decorative. Visitors are advised to stand where the Cinder Crown tells them to stand, which is usually somewhere with a lovely view and a very short walk to the door.

Kingdom II · Water

Sapphire Lake

“A splashy kingdom with very polite tides.”

A mirror-lake so still that the Ripple Court holds its meetings on the reflection and considers the sky to be a junior partner. Lily pads serve as market stalls, committee rooms and, after hours, trampolines. The lake's healers are famously generous and the lake's tides famously well-mannered: they arrive, they announce themselves, and they never take anything that was not offered.

Mud Mountain

“Big rocks, soft moss, and the best mud around.”

Mud Mountain is a lumpy, cheerful heap of a kingdom that has been described in official surveys as “technically asleep.” Its people are in no rush, its puddles are legendary, and its fortifications are less built than grown. The Mossy Hold has never lost a war of attrition and has never quite understood why anyone would want to win one quickly.

Cloudtop Skyhold

“A windy keep with the softest front door in the sky.”

Cloudtop is a sky island that drifted into a pile of clouds one afternoon and decided to stay for the view. Nobody is entirely sure where the kingdom ends and the weather begins; the Breeze Roost maintains that this is the point. It is the fastest hearth and the most fragile, which its residents consider a fair trade for always being first to the snack table.

Radiant Heaven

“A warm kingdom that leaves the porch light on.”

Golden stairs, soft clouds, and a gate polished so thoroughly that it has its own opinions about weather. Radiant Heaven is the kingdom that remembers everyone's birthday and the kingdom that will absolutely bring it up during a Little War. Its beams are bright, its manners brighter, and its porch light has not been switched off in recorded history.

Moonlit Underworld

“A shadowy kingdom with a surprising amount of glow.”

A cozy underworld of violet crystals and purple fireflies, lit by a moon that the Gloom Court keeps in a jar and takes out for special occasions. The Underworld's reputation for menace is entirely undeserved and carefully maintained. Its pals do steal health when they hit things, but they always say thank you, and they always leave a snack.

Chapter Four

Where it is settled

Four agreed formats. The Compact permits all of them and quietly prefers the ones that finish before supper.

🛤️

Simple Lanes

Classic

Three to five raised platforms, one gatehouse per lane, no cleverness permitted. Pals walk straight ahead because that is what lanes are for.

  • Straight, readable gate race
  • No jumping, no lane switching
  • The format the Compact was written for
🌀

Shifting Maze

Shift

A labyrinth anywhere from eight-by-eight to twenty-five-by-twenty-five, square or gloriously rectangular. Every twelve to fifteen seconds the walls move, and they do not nudge — the shift cuts the route you were using, so both armies have to find a new one. Every third shift is a GRAND SHIFT and rebuilds even harder.

  • Pathfinding pals, one shared gate
  • Each shift replaces most of the route
  • No shift can seal or strand a pal
  • Route length, shift timer and a gold trail in the HUD
🏰

Tower Defence

Waves

A twelve-by-eight switchback with twelve build pads and fourteen opening spark. Kills pay spark back and a clean kill streak raises the bounty, so the economy is the siege: spend, level up, reposition, and cover the right bend. Eight waves, with bosses on four and eight.

  • Kill streak bounty ×1.2, ×1.4, ×1.6
  • Choose Laser, Slow, AOE or Troops per tower
  • Cheap level-ups with live stat previews · sell for 75% back
⚔️

Tower Clash

Capture

Seven hubs, two directional rails, and both sides choose. Pick NORTH or SOUTH; the rails meet at the Master, then split toward the rival keep. Break a tower and it is instantly yours.

  • Both sides choose a rail
  • Break a tower and it flips to you
  • The Master Tower opens the rival keep
